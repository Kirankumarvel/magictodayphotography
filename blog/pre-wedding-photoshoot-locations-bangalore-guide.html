Ethical photography and permits

A good frame is worth it. Good manners matter more.

Every couple wants a setting that says something about them. Bangalore offers misty lakes, granite heritage buildings, painted lanes and quiet quarries. What most couples don't realise is that many of these spots need written permission for a professional shoot.

Below you'll find ten locations we shoot at regularly. For each one we've listed the best timing, the permit you'll need, and what to keep in mind so you and your photographer leave no trace. Prices and rules do change, so treat this as a starting point, not the final word.

01 Church Street -painted walls and city energy

Best time: 7 to 9 AM for soft light and empty streets. Permission: the sidewalk is public, so no permit needed, but talk to the cafes if you want interior shots. Keep in mind: no loud music, keep tripod legs off walkways, and don't block shop entrances.

Tip: the pastel wall near The Bookshop gives the best natural bounce for portraits.

02 Hesaraghatta Lake -still water and winter birds

6:30–8:30best window, morning
200+bird species across the year
450 acresof reservoir and shoreline
Yesforest department permission

Built in 1894, this reservoir pulls in more than thirty thousand migratory birds between November and February. The rocky shoreline gives clean reflections and a naturally diffused light in the morning. Permission: forest and tourism department. Keep in mind: no drones, stay fifty metres away from nesting areas, and carry every piece of trash back with you.

Tip: a 70 to 200mm lens handles the bird silhouettes, and a polarising filter cuts the glare off the water.

03 Vidhana Soudha -heritage stone, empty at dawn

4,500 tonsof granite used in the building
6–7 AMquietest time for the public
Sun 6–8:30 PMfloodlit facade

Early morning is the simplest time to shoot here. The area is quiet, so you won't interfere with government work or daily foot traffic. On Sunday evenings the floodlights make for dramatic night shots, but you will need a tripod. Permission: Bangalore City Police approval for professional gear. Keep in mind: no blocking roads, no disrupting pedestrians.

Tip: the public terrace opposite Vidhana Soudha gives an elevated angle without needing any special access.

04 Quarry Sunset Point, Muddayyanapalya -golden hour drama

5:30–6:30 PMgolden hour window
~900melevation gives open horizon
Nosafety barriers, watch edges

A hidden spot with white rock formations and green water. The colour window is short, so arrive early and scout your frame in advance. Permission: inform the local panchayat before any commercial shoot. Keep in mind: stay at least ten feet from the quarry edge, carry all trash out, and avoid weekends when the area gets crowded.

Tip: arrive 45 minutes early. Rock platforms can crumble at the edges and you want to know which ones are safe before the light is right.

05 Madiwala Lake, BTM Layout -quiet water in the middle of the city

150+species of birds and fish
2.5 kmwalking track around the lake
Rs 10entry per person

Restored in 2016, the lake now hosts over three hundred resident egrets and herons. Misty mornings give a soft, painterly look that photographs well. Permission: Lake Development Authority. Keep in mind: stay on the walking path, don't feed the birds, and keep voices low.

Tip: the wooden viewing deck near the boathouse gives an unobstructed reflection of the water and treeline.

06 ZEN Park, Nagarbhavi -a meditation zone, not a set

20 ftBuddha statue at the centre
Silentno music, no amplified sound
WrittenBBMP permission required

The Japanese-themed garden does not allow professional pre-wedding shoots without written BBMP permission. Even when permission is granted, keep the group small, avoid blocking pathways, and go with silence. This is a place people come to sit with themselves. Treat it that way.

Tip: weekday mornings have the fewest visitors. Skip any artificial music or lighting setup.

07 Jayanagar Parks -green pockets near the metro

100 mfrom the nearest metro station
1,200trees inside the mini forest
40+bird species spotted regularly

Two of the better spots here are B.N. Vijayakumar Park and the mini forest next to it. Both work well for a half-shoot before moving somewhere bigger. Best time: 6:30 to 8:30 AM. Permission: BBMP permit. Keep in mind: stay on the walking tracks, don't climb trees, and give way to morning walkers who were here before you.

Tip: the arched bamboo tunnel near the mini forest entrance is worth using as a frame-within-a-frame.

08 Bannerghatta Hill -butterfly park and quiet hillside

90butterfly species at the park
650 melevation, cool air
Rs 500–2000forest department fees

India's first butterfly park opened here in 2006 and it's still the standout feature. The surrounding hills give you forest light without needing to trek far. Permission: forest department. Keep in mind: no flash around animals, stay on marked trails, and don't litter.

Tip: a macro lens plus an early start means dew on the flowering plants and butterflies that hold still for a few seconds longer than usual.

09 MG Road -an urban night backdrop

2 kmof heritage stretch
200+LED streetlights along the road
50k+daily foot traffic

Shoot early morning between 7 and 9 AM or after 7 PM. The mix of glass facades and streetlights gives you something that feels closer to editorial than wedding. Permission: tripods on public footpaths may need a police nod. Keep in mind: don't block shop entrances, watch for traffic, and be aware of the crowds after dark.

Tip: the glass front of the Methodist Church reflects a soft, controlled light for night portraits.

10 A spot near you -your terrace, your garden, your street

Zerotravel time for both of you
100%unique to your story
RWAconsent needed in most societies

Your terrace, a society garden, or a tree-lined avenue near your home. This is often the spot couples remember most because it's where they actually live. Permission: a written NOC from the RWA or local municipality usually covers it. Keep in mind: shoot on weekdays between 10 AM and 5 PM, let neighbours know, keep music off, and clean up before you leave.

Tip: bedsheets work as diffusers on bright days. Fairy lights and the blue hour after sunset make something that looks expensive but isn't.

Golden hour and lighting, in plain terms

The golden hour is the first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset. The light is warm, directional and soft on faces. Overcast days act like a giant softbox and give even skin tones without much fuss. If you're shooting against backlight, a silver reflector bounces light back into the shadows. Avoid the flat midday sun between 10 AM and 3 PM. If you can't avoid it, find open shade under a tree and work there.

Planning your shoot

Book at least four to six weeks ahead for regular dates. For peak season from October to February, book eight to ten weeks ahead. Popular photographers in Bangalore sell out during this window, and last-minute bookings usually mean limited location and time slots.

Two to three locations in a day is comfortable. Bangalore traffic makes anything more rushed and stressful. A typical plan starts at a park or lake at sunrise, moves to an urban or heritage spot mid-morning, and ends at a rooftop or terrace for golden hour.

The two best windows are 6:30 to 8:30 AM right after sunrise and 5:30 to 6:30 PM during golden hour. Midday between 10 AM and 3 PM gives harsh, flat light and is best avoided. Overcast afternoons work surprisingly well because the clouds act as a giant softbox.

Weather and season

October to February is the best window. Temperatures stay between 15 and 28 degrees, skies are clear, and the morning and evening light is soft. June to September brings rain but also dramatic skies for couples who want moody photos. March to May is hot and best avoided unless you shoot indoors.

Yes, and many couples do. Monsoon shoots work if you plan around short showers, use umbrellas as props and pick a location with covered areas nearby. Clear umbrellas photograph well and wet ground reflects light beautifully. We keep an indoor backup option ready for every monsoon shoot.

Bangalore weather can turn quickly. If rain is forecast we can shift to a nearby indoor venue such as a heritage bungalow, a studio or a covered courtyard, or move the shoot to another day that works for you. Your deposit stays valid either way and there is no rescheduling fee.

Permissions and rules

It depends on the location. Public streets and open plazas are usually fine for a small two-person shoot. Lakes, gardens, heritage buildings and government properties need written permission from the relevant authority. Vidhana Soudha needs police approval. Madiwala and Hesaraghatta need lake or forest department permits.

Security staff can stop the shoot, ask you to leave, and in some cases file a complaint. Authorities at Vidhana Soudha and BBMP-managed parks are strict about this. It is not worth risking a fine or a spoiled day. Always confirm the rule before the shoot and get any approval in writing.

Drone use is restricted in most of Bangalore. No-fly zones cover the airport, defence areas, Vidhana Soudha and the central business district. Flying without a permit can lead to confiscation and legal action. We do not use drones for pre-wedding shoots inside city limits and will advise you on what is legal for the specific location.

Style and preparation

Most couples get the best result from a mix. We shoot candid frames during movement and conversation, and pose a smaller set of portraits for family and framing. The candid shots usually end up being the ones you print, while the posed shots give you clean images for invitations and social media.

Get a full night's sleep, avoid alcohol the day before and eat a proper meal before you leave home. Bring a small bag with touch-up makeup, blotting paper, a change of clothes and comfortable shoes for walking between spots. Reach the first location ten minutes early so you are settled before the light is right.

Two or three outfits work well for a four to six hour shoot. Start with something formal such as a lehenga or a suit, switch to coordinated western wear for the middle section, and end with a flowy outfit for golden hour. Changing takes about twenty minutes each time, so plan the day around that.

We do not include a makeup artist in the package, but we can recommend two or three freelancers in Bangalore who work well with our style. On-location makeup and hair styling usually adds Rs 6,000 to Rs 15,000 depending on the look. Many couples hire an artist for the first two hours and do touch-ups themselves after.
